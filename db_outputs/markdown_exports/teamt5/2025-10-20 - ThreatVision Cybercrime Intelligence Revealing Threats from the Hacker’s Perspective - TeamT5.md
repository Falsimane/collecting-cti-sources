As ransomware and data breach incidents surge, cybercrime has become one of the most critical challenges to the global digital economy. Hacker groups today attack not only governments and large corporations but also industries such as healthcare, finance, and manufacturing—and even the critical infrastructure that supports daily life.

No matter the country, region, or sector, every organization faces escalating risks. No one is exempt.

## The Underground Is Closer Than You Think

Many still believe cybercrime happens in distant corners of the internet, but reality tells a different story. Stolen patient records from hospitals, banking credentials sold on the dark web, and leaked government data all directly affect our daily lives and work. Clues of these incidents—forum posts, ransomware group statements, or transactions on underground markets and Telegram channels—often appear long before the attacks become public.

The real challenge, therefore, is not the **lack of intelligence** , but the ability to **interpret the language of the criminal underground**. Without local linguistic and cultural context, vital information is easily misunderstood or overlooked. Understanding how hackers communicate, which tools they use, and which industries they target is essential to turning fragmented signals into actionable intelligence.

## Transforming Defense Through the Hacker’s Lens

Hackers plan their actions carefully—selecting targets, exchanging tools, and treating attacks as a business. Organizations that rely solely on traditional defense models can only respond after an attack occurs. By instead adopting the hacker’s perspective—understanding their motives, processes, and methods—defenders can predict potential moves and prepare ahead of time.

This mindset shift transforms cybersecurity from **reactive patching** to **adaptive defense** that evolves with the threat landscape.
